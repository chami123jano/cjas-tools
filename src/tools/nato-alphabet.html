NATO Phonetic Alphabet

For reading a reference number down the phone without repeating it four times.

The numbers are written normally but said oddly. The word is still "nine", but it is pronounced NIN-er, and four is FOW-er — because nine is easily heard as five over a bad line, and four as "for". The pronunciation column above is the part that matters when you are reading something out. Aviation and shipping stick to it; nobody will mind if you say the ordinary words.

Why this alphabet and not another. The words were chosen in the 1950s by testing them over deliberately bad radio links with speakers of several languages — which is why they sound odd. "Quebec" and "Juliett" are spelled the way they are on purpose, and Juliett keeps its double T so French speakers do not read the final letter as silent.
