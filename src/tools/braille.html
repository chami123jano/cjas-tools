Braille Translator

Type on either side. Letter-by-letter Unified English Braille, the standard for English in most of the world.

This is uncontracted Braille (Grade 1). Every letter gets its own cell. Most adult readers use contracted Braille (Grade 2), where common words and letter groups have shortcuts — "and" is a single cell. Grade 1 is what learners start on, and anyone who reads Grade 2 can read it too.

The three signs that are not letters. ⠠ before a letter makes it a capital, and two of them make the whole word capitals. ⠼ switches to numbers: the letters a to j then mean 1 to 0. ⠰ after a number means "letters again" when the next letter could be mistaken for a digit.

To print it for a reader, the dots have to be raised. Ordinary printers cannot do that, so this is for learning, labelling with a Braille label maker, or sending to someone with a Braille display.
