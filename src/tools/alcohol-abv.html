The three scales. ABV is the percentage of the drink that is alcohol, and it is what labels in Sri Lanka and most countries show. US proof is simply double the ABV. The old British proof came from the strength at which gunpowder soaked in the spirit would still light: 100° proof is 57.15% ABV, so British proof is ABV × 1.75.

"Standard drink" means different amounts in different countries. The World Health Organization uses 10 g of pure alcohol, which is what is shown here, and the UK counts in units of 8 g. Alcohol weighs 0.789 g per ml. These figures are arithmetic from the label, not advice about how much is safe to drink.
