A run of six heads is not unusual. In a hundred flips there is roughly an eight in ten chance of a run of five or more somewhere in it. People expect heads and tails to alternate more than they really do, which is why a fair coin so often feels broken.

And the next flip is still fifty-fifty. However long the run, the coin has no memory of it. The percentages above drift towards fifty over hundreds of flips, not because anything corrects itself, but because one unusual stretch gets diluted by everything after it.

Each flip uses your browser's secure randomness, with a rejection step so neither side is favoured.
