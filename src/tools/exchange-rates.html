These are mid-market rates, not bank rates. They are the reference rate between two currencies, which is the number you see quoted in the news. A bank or exchange house will always give you less when you sell and charge more when you buy — the gap is how they make their money, and on remittances it is usually 1 to 3 rupees per dollar.

Use this to sanity-check what you are being offered, not as the rate you will actually get. For that, ask the bank.

This is one of the few tools here that needs the internet. The last rates fetched are kept on your device, so it still shows you something when you are offline — it just tells you how old it is.
